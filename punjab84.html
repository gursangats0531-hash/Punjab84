Punjab
1984

JUNE — OCTOBER 1984 & BEYOND

A factual, respectful record of a year that became a major turning point in the modern history of Punjab and in the lives of many people across India.

Context

Understanding 1984

The events of 1984 cannot be understood as a single incident. They emerged from political tensions, demands for greater autonomy, armed militancy, state action, and escalating communal violence in the early 1980s.

BEFORE 1984

Political tensions

Punjab experienced growing political conflict around questions of federalism, language, religion, regional rights and the distribution of resources.

EARLY 1980s

Militancy & violence

Armed Sikh militant groups committed serious violence against civilians and public figures, including attacks affecting both Sikh and Hindu communities.

1984

A turning point

Military action at the Golden Temple, the assassination of Indira Gandhi and subsequent anti-Sikh violence profoundly changed Punjab and India.

June 1984

Operation Blue Star

In June 1984, the Indian Army entered the Golden Temple complex in Amritsar to remove armed militants who had taken positions there. The operation caused extensive damage to the complex and resulted in the deaths of civilians, pilgrims, militants and security personnel. [Sources: Human Rights Watch]

3 JUNE

Curfew and military deployment

Punjab was placed under a curfew as military forces moved into the state amid escalating tensions.

4–6 JUNE

Military operation

The assault on the Golden Temple complex, commonly known as Operation Blue Star, took place during these days. The fighting caused heavy casualties and damage to the complex. [Source: Human Rights Watch]

AFTER JUNE

Deepening grief and anger

The events intensified anger and alienation among many Sikhs and became a central part of collective memory surrounding 1984.

October–November 1984

Assassination & anti-Sikh violence

On 31 October 1984, Prime Minister Indira Gandhi was assassinated by two Sikh members of her security staff. In the days that followed, organized attacks against Sikhs occurred in Delhi and other parts of India. Human Rights Watch reports that at least 2,733 Sikhs were killed in Delhi during the violence. [Source: Human Rights Watch]

31 OCTOBER 1984

Indira Gandhi assassinated

The assassination was followed by widespread violence against Sikhs, particularly in Delhi.

1–3 NOVEMBER

Mass violence

Sikh homes, businesses and places of worship were attacked. Thousands of people were killed across India, with Delhi experiencing some of the worst violence. [Source: Human Rights Watch]

AFTERWARDS

Justice & accountability

Multiple commissions and committees examined the violence. Human Rights Watch has documented serious failures in investigations and accountability. [Source: Human Rights Watch]

Important: Punjab84 presents 1984 as a historical subject requiring careful sourcing. The suffering of victims and families should be treated with dignity, while claims about responsibility should be supported by reliable documentary evidence.
The years after

Legacy of 1984

The violence and conflict did not end with 1984. Human Rights Watch has documented serious human-rights abuses during counterinsurgency operations in Punjab from 1984 to 1995, alongside serious abuses committed by Sikh militant groups. [Sources: Human Rights Watch]

Victims & families

Families continued to seek truth, recognition, compensation and accountability for deaths, disappearances and other abuses.

Memory

1984 remains deeply significant in Sikh communities and in discussions about Punjab's modern history, identity and human rights.

Historical record

Official inquiries, court records, eyewitness accounts, journalism and human-rights documentation provide important—sometimes contested—sources for research.
